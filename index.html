Narration transcript
  1. Genome-wide studies find thousands of risk variants. But association alone does not tell you the mechanism.
  2. Behind every GWAS signal is a person with a genetic predisposition, and disease can arise in one organ or in several.
  3. Disease happens in tissue, and cells don't act alone. Spatial omics maps each organ's cells within their microenvironment, or niche, from the layers of the brain to the follicles of a lymph node.
  4. Grouped by gene expression, cells form types that behave differently across disease states and stages. Among fifty-seven cell types, regulatory T cells showed the strongest enrichment of type 1 diabetes genetic risk.
  5. Placed back in each tissue, cell types show which neighbourhoods they share.
  6. Folded chromatin brings regulatory elements into contact with genes, nominating candidates, including distant ones.
  7. Most risk variants sit in cis-regulatory elements, such as enhancers, that loop across the chromatin to reach the genes they control.
  8. CRISPR screens then test which predicted links actually change a gene. In one screen, chromatin loops had predicted only eight of the twenty-three genes that responded.
  9. Linked genes then meet existing drugs. For example, an inhibitor of FDFT1 shifted T-cell cytokines: a pharmacological hypothesis worth testing further.
  10. From a patient's concern to a hypothesis worth testing. This is the work of Khanh B. Trang.

About

Medicine taught me to start with the patient. Public health taught me that a finding is only as good as the population it holds for, and that confounding is the usual reason something looks true when it is not. My PhD in Lausanne mapped how nuclear receptors bind the liver genome across the day, at the bench and in code, and a summer at the Broad Institute’s Connectivity Map showed me how perturbation data can point to a drug’s mechanism.

At Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia I connect disease genetics to cell types, regulatory elements and candidate genes, using 3D chromatin, single-cell and spatial data, CRISPRi screens and causal inference. I mentor an MD/PhD student and build interactive tools so collaborators can query results directly.

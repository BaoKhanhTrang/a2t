How I integrate

Genetics prioritises variants. Multiomics places them in cells and links them to genes. My work is at the crossings, and at knowing where each crossing can fail.

The clinical and population lens

Choose the question MD
Clinical training shapes the phenotype before any model runs: what counts as a case, which comparison matters to a patient, which outcome would change care.
Design for the population MPH
Reference scores calibrated across populations. Endpoint transformations chosen for the estimand. Sample sizes set from the analysis, not from convenience.
Protect participants
Consent and analysis plans that state what genetic information is and is not returned, how long data are kept, and who may access them.

Is the difference real?

Tissue studies compare diseased and healthy samples, but disease can shift a whole organ, not just the lesion. Move the sliders. Synthetic data, for illustration.

Difference-in-differences assumes that, without the disease, lesion and adjacent tissue would differ by the same amount in cases as in controls. When that holds, it isolates the lesion-specific change; the naive comparison cannot.

Practices

  • QC rules for single-cell and spatial data written as automated pass/warn/fail checks, so a pipeline stops rather than quietly passes.
  • Reference panels identity-checked before any LD-dependent estimate.
  • Every quoted number traced to the file that produced it.
  • Ongoing work: atlas-scale single-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics, ambient-RNA and doublet modelling, deconvolution benchmarking, drug-target Mendelian randomisation, paired chromatin-accessibility and 3D-genome analyses.
